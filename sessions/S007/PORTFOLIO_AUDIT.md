## Independently checked structural obstruction (elementary, not novel)
Let `B_k={F_x : x∈{0,1}^k}` with disjoint coordinate blocks `{(i,0),(i,1)}` and `F_x={(i,x_i):1≤i≤k}`. Every edge is size k and `|B_k|=2^k`. For any r≥3, an r-sunflower on distinct F_x would require each coordinate's selected symbols to be all equal or pairwise distinct; two symbols make pairwise distinct impossible for r≥3, and all-equal in every coordinate would make the sets identical. Hence B_k is r-sunflower-free. It has VC-dimension exactly k: fix `T={(i,0):i≤k}`, and every subset of T is the trace of some F_x (and no k-uniform family can shatter k+1 points). For a subfamily G⊆B_k, its traces on T are **injective**, so if VC(G)≤d, Sauer–Shelah gives `|G|≤Σ_{j=0}^{d} binom(k,j)`. For fixed d, the retained mass fraction is ≤`2^{−k}Σ_{j=0}^d binom(k,j)→0`. Therefore any plan to take a constant fraction of *every* sunflower-free F into a bounded-VC subfamily (then appeal to Ge et al.) is **false**, even for the canonical 2^k example. This only refutes THAT transfer, not the conjecture or every possible VC-sensitive decomposition. It is an elementary consequence of classical results, NO novel theorem claimed.
